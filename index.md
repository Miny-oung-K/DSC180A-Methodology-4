**What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**

The most interesting topic to me is how sound can be broken down, reconstructed, and controlled through movement. In Harmonic Lab, I explored how changing frequencies, amplitudes, and harmonics changes waveforms and vowel sounds. As a violinist, I enjoyed connecting these ideas to Air Violin, where hand and facial gestures control musical sounds and timbre. Sound Interpolation Explorer was especially interesting because it let me explore a continuous transition between two recorded mouth sounds, such as an open-mouth "AH" and a closed-mouth "MM." Using AI to help build and revise these interfaces has allowed me to turn musical ideas into interactive experiments.

**Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**

For Quarter 2, I would like to investigate whether more detailed full-body tracking can make sound interpolation more expressive and intuitive. I would extend the hand and face controls in Air Violin to track more body landmarks, including the shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, and ankles. My goal would be to recreate the mouth-sound transitions I explored in Sound Interpolation Explorer through body movement. For example, moving from an open posture to a closed posture could control the transition from "AH" to "MM," aiming to produce a continuous intermediate sound rather than simply playing both recordings together. I would start with a few reliable movements and compare a simple control based on hand position with controls using several joint angles. I would evaluate how smoothly the sound changes, how quickly it responds, and how easily users can reach and repeat an intended sound.

**What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**

One change I would make is to connect the strongest parts of my three prototypes into one interface. Harmonic Lab helped me understand sound structure, Air Violin explored camera-based control, and Sound Interpolation Explorer focused on transitions between recorded sounds. Combining these components would let me test how movement controls interpolation directly. I would also add a calibration step and a visible landmark overlay so users can see which movements the camera detects. Based on my experience revising Air Violin when certain hand gestures were difficult to capture, I would prioritize a small number of dependable controls before adding more joints and more complicated mappings.

**What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**

I would be interested in using existing AI-based body landmark tracking tools to capture more detailed movement without training a new model myself. I would explore joint angles and relative distances so the controls depend less on where someone stands in the camera frame. Motion smoothing could help reduce jitter, while continuous mappings could turn changes in posture into gradual changes in sound. On the audio side, I would like to explore spectral interpolation and filtering to better reproduce the transition between mouth sounds. I would continue using AI-assisted coding to develop and revise the interface, along with listening tests and usability testing to check whether the resulting sounds and controls match my intentions.
